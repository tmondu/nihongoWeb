Verbs are the engine of every Japanese sentence. Without them, you can't describe actions, express desires, or talk about what happened yesterday! The good news? Japanese verbs are actually more regular than English verbs – once you know the patterns, you can conjugate thousands of verbs correctly.

Let's master the 50 most essential Japanese verbs together! 🚀

<InfoBox type='success'>
  These 50 verbs appear constantly in daily Japanese conversation and cover 80%
  of common actions. Master them, and you'll understand most basic sentences!
</InfoBox>

## Japanese Verb Basics 📚

### The Three Verb Groups

Japanese verbs are divided into three groups based on how they conjugate:

  <thead>

    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td>Group 2</td>
      <td>一段動詞 (Ichidan)</td>

    <tr>

  </tbody>
</table>

<InfoBox type='tip'>
  As a beginner, focus on masu form for speaking – it's polite and safe in any
  situation. Learn dictionary form for understanding and later conjugations.
</InfoBox>

## Conjugation Patterns 📊

### Group 1 (U-verbs) Pattern

Change the last う sound to い, then add ます:

<table>
  <thead>
    <tr>
      <th>Ending</th>
      <th>Change</th>
      <th>Example</th>
    </tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr>
      <td>う</td>
      <td>い</td>
      <td>買う → 買います</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td>く</td>
      <td>き</td>
      <td>書く → 書きます</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td>す</td>
      <td>し</td>
      <td>話す → 話します</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td>つ</td>
      <td>ち</td>
      <td>待つ → 待ちます</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td>む</td>
      <td>み</td>
      <td>読む → 読みます</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td>る</td>
      <td>り</td>
      <td>帰る → 帰ります</td>
    </tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

### Group 2 (Ru-verbs) Pattern

Drop る and add ます: 食べる → 食べます

### Group 3 (Irregular)

Memorize: する → します, 来る → 来ます

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if a verb is Group 1 or Group 2?

Most verbs ending in -eru or -iru are Group 2. Exceptions include 帰る, 走る, 入る, 知る. When in doubt, check a dictionary!

### What's the most important verb to learn first?

する (suru)! It's used in countless compound verbs: 勉強する, 料理する, 運転する – they all use する.

### Why does 知る work differently?

知る describes the action of "finding out." For "knowing" (a state), use 知っています.
